# Creative Direction Engine

This is not a style generator. It converts a screenplay-specific viewing problem into one coherent formal system. Use it before detailed shot planning.

## 1. Diagnose The Viewing Problem

Write one sentence in this form:

```text
The episode must make the audience [feel/anticipate/misread/understand] ______ while controlling access to ______ until ______ changes.
```

Then identify:

- the character, force, place, object, or system that owns attention at the opening;
- what the audience knows earlier than, with, or later than each material character;
- the episode's decisive change in intimacy, control, scale, danger, trust, or certainty;
- the beat that must feel irreversible;
- what emotional or sensory residue should remain after the final image or sound.

If the answer is only `紧张`, `电影感`, `高级`, `沉浸`, or `有冲击力`, the problem is not yet specific enough.

## 2. Write The Neutral Baseline

Describe the simplest feasible AI-generated results that preserve performance, geography, events and exact dialogue. Estimate calls, generated seconds, asset additions and external assembly before elaborating cinematography. Traditional coverage patterns are not a default shopping list.

Example shape, not fixed wording:

```text
Neutral baseline: group compatible action, dialogue, reactions and results into complete generated units; use a native edited result where the source calls for compression. Reuse available assets and add independent acquisition only for an indispensable result.
```

The final plan may use the baseline. A more elaborate system must produce a concrete story or audience gain.

## 3. Develop Distinct Candidate Systems When Useful

When the episode supports materially different readings, develop two or three candidates. Do not generate alternatives ceremonially when one solution is already clear.

Each candidate must define:

```text
观看权：who or what owns perception
信息关系：audience ahead / aligned / behind, and when this changes
距离制度：intimate / social / remote / variable, and why
空间制度：stable / partial / withheld / expanding / collapsing map
运动制度：what earns camera or actor movement; what earns stillness
时间制度：continuous / compressed / suspended / fragmented / repeated
剪辑制度：continuity / collision / parallel pressure / reaction ownership / deliberate absence
声音制度：what leads, carries, contradicts, disappears, or returns
母题及变化：what repeats and how meaning changes
高潮变形：which established rule breaks or reaches its strongest form
AI执行路径：continuous/native edited results, necessary extra calls and seconds, reusable/new assets, manual assembly and conditional fallback triggers
```

Candidates must differ in audience relationship, not merely lens choice or camera support.

Useful contrasts include:

- audience aligned with a limited character versus audience granted superior spatial knowledge;
- stable observational distance versus progressive bodily enclosure;
- clear geography followed by controlled spatial collapse versus withheld geography followed by revelation;
- continuous duration versus editorial compression and collision;
- human-owned perception versus system, surveillance, machine, crowd, or environmental observation;
- sound-led threat versus image-led discovery.

## 4. Compare And Choose

Compare each viable candidate against the neutral baseline:

| Criterion | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Story gain | What becomes clearer, tenser, funnier, more painful, or more surprising? |
| Character truth | Does the form express the actual relationship and information permissions? |
| Whole-episode arc | Can the system develop rather than repeat unchanged? |
| Distinctiveness | Could the same paragraph be pasted onto a different episode? |
| Editability | Can the intended reveal, hold, collision, and transition be assembled? |
| Production truth | Can required results be generated without weakening events or overpacking the capacity? |
| Production burden | What calls, generated seconds, new assets and user work does this add over the simplest viable baseline, and what concrete payoff justifies them? |
| Removal test | What meaningful loss occurs if the method is removed? |

Choose one primary system. Keep one contrast or exception only when it makes the primary system legible. Do not blend every attractive candidate.

## 5. Create A Formal Arc

A directing idea becomes an episode system only when it changes with story state. Map:

```text
Opening rule:
First meaningful variation:
Midpoint pressure or reversal:
Climax mutation or rupture:
Aftershock residue:
Final image/sound relationship:
```

The climax does not automatically require faster cutting, wider scale, or more camera movement. It may earn stillness, silence, duration, withheld reaction, or a sudden return to an earlier form.

## 6. Build Motif Through Transformation

A motif can be spatial, gestural, graphic, temporal, material, bodily, mechanical, or sonic. It is useful only if repetition changes meaning, ownership, scale, control, or emotional charge.

For every motif, answer:

- first appearance and initial meaning;
- later return and changed context;
- climax or ending transformation;
- whether the viewer will perceive it without explanation;
- whether the required shot or sound can actually be acquired.

Do not turn every recurring prop or color into a motif.

## 7. Defeat Generic Direction

Reject or revise when:

- the concept is mostly adjectives;
- every scene receives a different stylish device without a governing change;
- the plan defaults to pushing in on emotion and handheld action regardless of context;
- lens numbers appear without spatial or perceptual purpose;
- cutting gets faster merely because danger increases;
- silence, slow motion, shallow focus, or match cuts are treated as automatic prestige;
- character performance is replaced by camera movement;
- the climax only adds scale rather than changing audience relationship;
- the idea cannot be paraphrased as a change in what the viewer feels, knows, or anticipates.
