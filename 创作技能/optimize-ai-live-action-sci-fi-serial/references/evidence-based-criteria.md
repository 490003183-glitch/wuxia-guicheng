# Evidence-based audit criteria

This is an editorial review framework, not a validated audience prediction model. Verify current policy, platform, model and audience claims when the task depends on them.

## Evidence hierarchy

Use the highest applicable level and state the level in the report.

1. `A` Project canon and finalized script: authoritative for what the story is allowed to be.
2. `B` Creator backend behavioral data: best available evidence for this account's actual viewing behavior.
3. `C` Official policy/platform/model material and well-scoped institutional research.
4. `D` Peer-reviewed or transparent academic research with method limits stated.
5. `E` Same-series public performance and scene-level observation.
6. `F` Cross-account cases, comments, trade reporting, and expert opinion.
7. `G` Intuition or analogy: hypothesis only.

Never let a lower level overrule a higher-level canon fact. Canon authority does not prove audience effectiveness; keep those questions separate.

## The three-layer model

Audit three different outcomes. Do not collapse them into one “爽不爽” judgment.

### Entry promise

The opening should let a feed-discovered viewer perceive a concrete abnormality, goal, conflict, or question. A spectacle counts only if it belongs to this episode's problem.

### Episode state change

The episode should change at least one material state: safety, evidence, permission, equipment, relationship, location, identity knowledge, power control, or commitment. A cliffhanger does not replace payoff.

### Serial/IP continuation

The episode should feed a repeatable engine: escalating missions, rule puzzles, relationship debt, ability cost, institutional conflict, destination progress, or antagonist action. A one-off bigger monster is not automatically a serial engine.

### Once-only milestones and escalation debt

Record major events that cannot deliver the same first-time value again: first activation, first real battle, first kill, first public reveal, first irreversible betrayal, first relationship crossing, first uncontrolled ability, or first permanent equipment loss.

Do not penalize a climax merely for using several milestones. Report two separate findings:

1. whether the current episode or chapter earns and delivers them;
2. what future escalation debt is created after those firsts are spent.

A repeatable continuation may escalate through cost, control, enemy adaptation, institutional response, relationship consequences, information asymmetry, or harder rule combinations. “A larger version of the same fight” is only one option and often the least distinctive one. Use no universal count threshold.

## Ten dimensions

Score only for localization: `0` absent/severe, `1` weak, `2` functional, `3` clear, `N/A` not applicable or insufficient material. Always include evidence and confidence.

### E — Entry

- How soon does a problem requiring attention appear?
- Is the entry promise honestly connected to the episode?
- Does the viewer know why this must be watched now?

### T — Task and stakes

- Can a new viewer say who must do what?
- What resists the goal?
- What changes if the attempt fails?

### M — Mechanism in action

- Does a sci-fi rule alter the next action?
- Is exposition immediately tested, used, or contradicted?
- Can the viewer use the rule to predict danger or a choice?

### P — Payoff

- What is obtained, lost, solved, transferred, exposed, or made irreversible?
- Is the payoff prepared by earlier information?
- Is some satisfaction delivered before the next hook?

### R — Re-entry

- Can a feed-discovered viewer understand the episode without homework?
- How many prior facts, names, parallel threads, reused versions, and timeline jumps are mandatory?
- Can context be rebuilt through action rather than a lecture?

### C — Character and relationship

- Does the protagonist choose rather than merely receive assignments?
- Do supporting characters have interests and boundaries?
- Is a relationship different at the end?
- Is human texture protected rather than compressed away?

### S — Science-fiction validity

- Are rule conditions stable and testable?
- Does spectacle alter tactics, cost, allegiance, or relationship?
- Does the mechanism intensify a human question?
- Distinguish unknown conditions from contradictions.

### G — AI-live-action feasibility

- Can beats fit the selected model and duration budget with a clear initial state?
- Are speaker, reaction, main action, props, spatial geometry, and end state clear?
- Are multi-person lip sync, contact, crowds, injury/wardrobe continuity, and dynamic realism acknowledged?

### H — Hook

- Is the ending a concrete new decision, threat, clue, cost, or adversary action?
- Does it follow from established causality?
- Does it create a larger question after a smaller one is answered?

### O — Originality and fatigue

- Does the episode repeat the same training, reveal, boss escalation, argument, or cliff device?
- Is the project's own institutional, relational, and sci-fi logic doing the work?
- Has a generic face-slap/hidden-identity formula displaced the characters?
- Which once-only milestones are consumed here, and does the continuation still have a different escalation axis?

## Hard gates

Do not offset these with scores:

- canon/time/version conflict;
- information-permission violation;
- unsupported character-voice or relationship change;
- science-fiction causal contradiction;
- violation of confirmed operational ceilings;
- unauthorized rewrite of finalized material;
- unverified compliance presented as fact;
- infeasible production treated as guaranteed;
- decisive claim from insufficient data.

## Eight payoff families

Identify the existing payoff before adding another:

1. ability execution;
2. rule-based problem solving;
3. authority/permission transfer;
4. relationship boundary change;
5. equipment acquisition or activation;
6. partial truth revelation;
7. survival/rescue/arrival;
8. character-specific moral or emotional choice.

“More intense” is not a payoff family. Name the state change.

## Exposition test

For each explanatory passage ask:

1. What current decision uses this information?
2. Does the next beat verify it?
3. If removed, can the current action still be understood?
4. Can an object, screen, permission result, body response, spatial change, or failure carry it?
5. Are more than three newly necessary terms introduced in one scene?

Do not delete necessary worldbuilding merely because it is exposition. Add a concrete present-tense anchor or distribute it across actions.

## Minimum-change ladder

- `L0` packaging/order/recap/version labeling;
- `L1` reorder existing beats;
- `L2` attach explanation to evidence/action/consequence;
- `L3` local scene or dialogue rewrite, with authorization for finalized text;
- `L4` structural rewrite, explicitly authorized.

## Public-performance inference rules

Allowed: form hypotheses from same-account, similar-time, similar-condition comparisons and label the sampling date.

Required confounds: followers, recommendation/paid distribution, title/cover, posting interval, duration, IP, adaptation recognition, contest support, collection, reposted/re-edited material, hiatus return, and consecutive-post cannibalization.

Forbidden: likes as retention; comments as representative satisfaction; cross-account absolute counts as script quality; one outlier as causality.
